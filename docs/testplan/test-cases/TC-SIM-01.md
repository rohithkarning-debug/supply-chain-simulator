### TC-SIM-01: Daily Step Order and Automatic Replenishment

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| TC ID | TC-SIM-01 |
| Title | The daily step advances time, delivers shipments after their lead time, fills back-orders after replenishment, reorders exactly once per item, and trims reorders to warehouse capacity |
| Requirement(s) | SCM-F-041, SCM-F-042 |
| Type | Integration |
| Preconditions | 1. Python 3.11 virtual environment with `pip install -r requirements.txt` completed. 2. Repository at commit `b3702ef` (or a later green `main`). 3. Unless stated otherwise, each step uses a new scenario: `Simulation(seed=1)` with supplier `SUP` (lead time 2 days, reliability 1.0, so never delayed), warehouse `WH` with capacity 1000, and item (`WH`, `SKU`) with policy `StockPolicy(reorder_point, reorder_quantity, 500)` supplied by `SUP`. |
| Test data | Made-up IDs as used in the test code: supplier `SUP`, warehouse `WH`, SKU `SKU`, customers `C1`, `C2`. Defaults: initial stock 50, reorder point 20, reorder quantity 100. Step-specific values are given in each step. |
| Objective | Prove that `Simulation.step()` runs the five daily stages in the required order (arrivals → today's orders → back-order retry → replenishment → report) and that automatic replenishment dispatches exactly one capacity-safe shipment per item that needs it. |
| Steps | 1. **Day counter:** with stock 50, read `day`; call `step()`; then `run(3)`; read `day` and the `day` of every report in `history`. 2. **Arrival after lead time:** with stock 100, `step()` (day 1); dispatch 40 units from `SUP` on day 1; `step()` (day 2) and read the level; `step()` (day 3) and read the shipment status, `actual_day` and level. 3. **Back-order then fulfilment:** with stock 30, schedule `C1` for 60 units on day 1; `step()`; schedule `C2` for 15 units on day 2; `step()`; `step()` (day 3); `step()` (day 4); read order `ORD-0001` and the level after each step. 4. **Exactly one reorder:** with stock 10 (below the reorder point), `run(1)` three times, reading `shipments.in_transit()` and `logs[d].reorders` after each day. 5. **Capacity trimming:** new `Simulation()` with supplier `SUP` (lead time 1), warehouse `WH` with capacity 50, policy `StockPolicy(20, 100, 50)`, stock 10; `run(3)`. |
| Expected result | 1. `day` is 0 before stepping; after `step()` it is 1 and the report has `"day": 1`; after `run(3)` it is 4 and the history days are `[1, 2, 3, 4]`. 2. The shipment's `expected_day` is 3; on day 2 the level is still 100; on day 3 the status is `DELIVERED`, `actual_day == 3` and the level is 140. 3. Day 1: `ORD-0001` is `BACKORDERED` and nothing is in transit (30 is above the reorder point). Day 2: the 15-unit order is fulfilled, stock falls to 15 and one replenishment is in transit. Day 3: `ORD-0001` is still `BACKORDERED`. Day 4: the 100 units arrive *before* the retry, so `ORD-0001` is `FULFILLED` with `fulfilled_day == 4` and the level is 15 + 100 − 60 = 55. 4. Day 1: exactly 1 shipment in transit and 1 reorder logged. Day 2: still 1 in transit and 0 new reorders. Day 3: it arrives, nothing is in transit, the level is 110, and the total number of reorders over the 3 days is 1. 5. The 100-unit reorder is trimmed to 40, no `CapacityExceededError` is raised on arrival, and the final level is exactly 50. |
| Pass/fail criteria | **Pass** if every expected value is observed and all pytest tests listed below pass. **Fail** if the day counter or history is wrong, a shipment arrives early or late, a back-order is not filled on the arrival day, a duplicate reorder is dispatched while one is in transit, or any arrival raises `CapacityExceededError`. |
